Two tools with similar-sounding names do very different things: a **voice rep timer** and **automatic rep detection**. Understanding the difference will help you pick the right tool and set the right expectations.

## What a voice rep timer does

A voice rep timer speaks numbers on a schedule you configure. You set the target count and the seconds between announcements, and the app says `1`, `2`, `3`, and so on at that cadence.

Voice Rep Counter is this kind of tool. The count is produced by a timer, not by observing your movement. You perform each repetition in time with the spoken numbers.

## What automatic rep detection does

Automatic rep detection tries to recognize when you actually complete a movement, typically using a camera, motion sensors, or a wearable. It counts reps it believes it observed, and it may estimate tempo or other movement data.

That is a different category of technology, and it comes with different accuracy and privacy trade-offs.

## Why the difference matters

With a voice rep timer, the count is deterministic: it follows your configured interval regardless of what your body does. If you miss a rep or pause, the count continues on its schedule.

With automatic detection, the count depends on whether the system correctly recognized each movement—which can vary with lighting, angle, sensor placement, and movement quality.

Voice Rep Counter does **not** claim to sense physical reps. It does not use a camera or motion sensors to detect movement, and it does not evaluate exercise form.

## Which one should you choose?

If you want a simple, predictable pacing aid that works offline with no account, a voice rep timer is a solid choice. If you specifically need movement recognition, you would be looking for a different kind of product—and you should evaluate its accuracy claims and privacy practices carefully.
