Push-ups are a bodyweight staple, but keeping a steady rhythm while you also focus on form can be harder than it looks. A spoken count can take the mental counting off your plate so you can concentrate on the movement.

## Why pacing matters

A consistent cadence keeps each repetition deliberate. When you count in your head, the count tends to speed up as you fatigue—or slows down when you lose focus. An external spoken count gives you the same interval every time.

Voice Rep Counter is a timer-based pacer: it speaks a number on a schedule you define. It does **not** watch your body or detect whether a rep was completed. You move in time with the voice.

## A simple setup

1. Open Voice Rep Counter and create a **Reps Counter** session.
2. Name it `Push-ups`.
3. Set the target to **10 repetitions**.
4. Choose **3 seconds** between numbers.
5. Press **Start** and follow the spoken count.

Three seconds between numbers is a reasonable starting point, but there is no universal correct value. If the pace feels too fast, raise the interval; if it feels too slow, lower it. The point is to find a cadence you can sustain with good form.

## How to use the count

Lower as you hear the number, reach the bottom under control, and press back up before the next number arrives. The count is a metronome, not a coach: it will not tell you if your back sags or your range of motion is short.

If you lose the rhythm, use **Pause**, reset your position, and **Resume** when you are ready. You can also stop the session early if you need to.

## Adding rest between sets

Create a separate **Rest Timer** session—for example 60 seconds—and place it after your push-up session. With sequential playback, the app can move from the rep count into the rest interval and on to the next set.

## Keep it safe

A timer-paced count does not assess form or fatigue. Progress gradually, and stop if something hurts. If you have health concerns, speak to a qualified professional before starting a new routine.
